Demonstration 2

Deceptive warning — dental workstation

A simulated scareware takeover of an operatory imaging station at Saguaro Family Dental, an invented practice. It is loud, it is meant to be, and it is fake from top to bottom.

Before you press the button

  1. Tell the room this is a simulation. The dark bar at the top of the screen stays visible the whole time and says so.
  2. There is a siren. Turn it off below if anyone is sound-sensitive or the room is small.
  3. The exit is Esc. In a real browser window it is Alt+F4; if a real page ignores that, Ctrl+Shift+Esc opens Task Manager.
  4. Every button inside the warning is inert — but clicking one spawns more windows, exactly as the real thing does. Let a student try it.

Options

Turn the flashing off for anyone photosensitive. The demonstration still works without it.

  • It filled the screen. Tabs, address bar and close button disappeared, so it stopped feeling like a web page and started feeling like the computer.
  • It counted down. The deadline exists to stop you walking down the hall to ask somebody.
  • It knew where it was. Your practice name, the operatory, the workstation, your patients' files scrolling past. All of it can be guessed, scraped from a website, or simply made up — and it is devastatingly convincing anyway.
  • It gave you one action. Call this number. No real security software has ever asked anyone to telephone anybody.
  • It punished clicking. Every button you pressed opened two more windows. That is deliberate: panic plus clicking is the state it wants you in.
  • It made noise in a room where a patient was listening — and embarrassment makes people act fast and quietly instead of asking for help.

None of it could stop Alt+F4, and none of it could stop you telling your office manager.
