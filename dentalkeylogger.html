The teaching point. Everything on the right happened before the Sign in button was pressed. A page that looks like a sign-in screen has already received what you typed the moment you typed it — there is no “undo” after the fact, and backspacing does not take it back. The padlock and https:// in the address bar say the connection is encrypted; they say nothing about who is on the other end of it.

If you ever do this on a real-looking page by mistake: stop, do not press submit, close the window with Alt+F4, change that password from a device you trust, and tell your office manager or IT contact right away. Speed matters more than embarrassment.
